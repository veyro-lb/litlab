## Explicit per-understanding acceptance checklist

The editorial pass described above requires **each of these items** to be addressed, not simply a paragraph count:

1. The official Guide's understanding and notes mapped to its correct stable ID.
2. Necessary biological background visible in the lesson rather than hidden behind Help.
3. Scientific terms explained in context or in an adjacent learner-facing term entry.
4. Relevant molecular/process mechanism (or explicitly not required for a descriptive standard).
5. An appropriate worked or biological example.
6. Correct shared versus additional-HL section gating.
7. Meaningful close-by figure, table, model or process diagram where useful, without ornamental filler.
8. Claims checked against Guide/coursebook and independently corroborated when the coursebook seems inconsistent; **a named qualified reviewer remains pending for all fifteen**.
9. No known required prescribed content omitted; off-syllabus material only when it clarifies an important distinction.
10. Independently written prose with source-traceable `claimReferences`; no source passages/figures copied.
11. At least one existing aligned selection, recall or formal question, testing the concept rather than mere heading recognition.

The current machine-readable schema reconstructs `explanation` from `teachingBlocks` and `claimReferences`. It can check mapping and presence but **cannot certify completeness, accuracy, licences, comprehension, teaching quality or correctness of rendered visuals**. The eight fixed teaching paragraphs added to the previous baseline in the six changed understandings are not a substitute for live learner evaluation.

## Important source discrepancy and editorial decisions

- The Hodder chapter's discussion of directionality (printed p. 25) appears to call the *coding* strand the transcription template. That usage conflicts with conventional strand definitions. The BioLab explanation consistently uses the **template/noncoding** DNA strand as the strand read by RNA polymerase, and explains why the **coding** strand usually matches RNA apart from T/U. This correction needs independent confirmation but the contradictory textbook wording must not be copied.
- The syllabus represents RNA polymerization as condensation, whereas enzymatic transcription uses activated ribonucleoside triphosphates and releases pyrophosphate. The lesson distinguishes these **two levels of description** instead of presenting the simplified water-loss equation as the complete enzyme mechanism.
- The Guide's term "limitless capacity" does not imply that any **finite** DNA molecule stores infinitely many bits. The existing section explains the unbounded increase in possible sequence diversity as molecule length can vary.
- The model 1KX5 does not establish the position of histone H1 in chromatin. The text explicitly states that limitation and does not restore a previously withheld inaccurate nucleosome figure.
- The Chargaff comparison contains *invented but clearly labelled calculated examples*, not purported primary measurements. The Hershey–Chase sequence is qualitative and reports no invented numeric radioactive counts.
