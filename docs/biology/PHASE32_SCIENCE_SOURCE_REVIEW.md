## Explicit discrepancies / cautionary findings

- **Essential amino acids:** an accessible third-party B1.2 Sirius-derived transcript/summary says humans synthesize nine of twenty and need the remaining eleven from food. This reverses the standard classification of **nine essential** and **eleven non-essential** for adult human nutrition, independently corroborated by NIH MedlinePlus: https://medlineplus.gov/ency/article/002222.htm and NCBI StatPearls: https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK557845/ . The official guide does **not** require specific amino-acid examples or the numerical count; LitLab explains the distinction without examinable-looking numbers.
- **Stereochemistry caution:** the same third-party transcript's shorthand around mirror-image amino-acid drawings risks wrongly treating stereoisomers as interchangeable. The LitLab diagram presents only a conventional generalized alpha-amino-acid structure, not a claim about stereochemistry.
- **Hodder dietary approximation:** printed p. 209 uses an approximate 'half' dietary amino-acid count; exact counts were independently corroborated rather than adopted. More importantly, the IB guide does not require memorizing the numbers.
- **Carbohydrate rings:** do not confuse a six-membered glucose ring (five carbons and one oxygen) with a molecule containing six carbons total. Counting ring corners alone is unreliable for pentose/hexose identification.
- **Starch and glycogen:** they are not identically branched throughout; glycogen is highly branched, while starch includes amylose and amylopectin. They are relatively, not absolutely, insoluble.
- **Cellulose:** covalent glycosidic bonds connect beta-glucose units along a chain; hydrogen bonding contributes between aligned chains. Abstract SVG symbols must not be mistaken for atomic structural formulae.
- **Fatty acids:** the double-bond/melting heuristic generally concerns **cis** unsaturation at comparable chain length and conditions; trans stereochemistry and chain length change packing. Never assert a universal melting-point law from C=C number alone.
- **ABO:** ABO carbohydrate antigens occur on surface glycoconjugates including glycoproteins and glycolipids. Never teach that every ABO determinant is exclusively part of protein.
- **Phospholipid head composition:** official simplified glycerol/two fatty acid/phosphate model is appropriate at this level; additional polar head attachments exist in real membrane phospholipids.
- **Steroid:** extensive non-polar fused rings favour permeability; do not say every steroid is absolutely non-polar or has no oxygen groups.
- **Denaturation:** pH/temperature can disrupt native structure without hydrolysing peptide bonds; reversibility varies. Do not promise that all proteins lose their structure permanently on heating.
- **Protein structure:** alpha/beta secondary patterns involve backbone hydrogen bonds; tertiary includes different R-group interactions, cysteine–cysteine disulfides and ionization-dependent attractions.
- **Conjugation versus quaternary:** haemoglobin has both multiple polypeptide subunits and non-protein haem groups. Distinct classification criteria. Insulin and collagen are examples of non-conjugated multi-chain structures described in the guide.
- **Data provenance:** B1.1 paper 1B contains an explicitly labelled *illustrative structural comparison*, not fabricated experimental measurements.
- **IB licensing:** the IB guide includes rights language concerning third-party commercial curriculum mapping. Actual publication/licensing or IP review remains a separate owner/legal gate. No IB approval is implied.
